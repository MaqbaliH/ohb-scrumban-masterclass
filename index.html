Persuasion Playbook

Word-for-Word Stakeholder Objection Scripts (Convincing OHB)

🏛️ 1. ExCom / MSSC / Product Sponsors

Slides 2, 6, 8, 9, 21

Hidden Fear: "Agile means no fixed dates or scope, and the team will use 'Agile' as an excuse when quarterly targets slip."

Your Pitch: "In our current Waterfall P4 phase, you wait 160 working days in a black box (Audit Finding F12) and discover delays only when UAT fails. Under Controlled Scrumban, we lock Committed Goals at Gate G3 backed by confirmed capacity and a protected Mandatory MVP boundary. Speculative Forecast and Pipeline Discovery items are separated so they never sabotage your Committed targets. And every two weeks at Sprint Review, you inspect working software—not a green slide deck that turns red before CAB."

🛡️ 2. Risk, ISO, Compliance, CAB & Internal Audit

Slides 3, 4, 7, 18

Hidden Fear: "Agile teams skip documentation, bypass security reviews, and deploy untested code without audit trails."

Your Pitch: "This model embeds Controls by Design across 6 formal Lifecycle Gates (G1–G6) and 8 workflow stages. No story starts without Entry Evidence (G4 DoR). No code moves to test without Maker-Checker peer review. Every story follows validation Route A, B, or C. Nothing reaches production without G5 (UAT Accepted) and G6 (Release Ready — VAPT, Rollback Plan, CAB approval). It directly resolves Audit Findings F12, F15, and F17."

🎯 3. Business Leads / Product Owners

Slides 9, 10, 12, 13

Hidden Fear: "If I can't email a developer directly mid-Sprint, my department's priorities will lose out to other departments."

Your Pitch: "When every PO pushes 'urgent' work directly onto developers, engineers context-switch 5 times a day and nothing finishes. By routing work through Weekly Replenishment into a WIP-limited Shared Ready Queue, once the team pulls your story, they finish it before starting more. You own 100% of your Product Backlog's order, and our Cross-Product Change Rule protects your Committed work from being displaced by another PO without formal escalation."

💻 4. Developers, Tech Leads & QA

Slides 12, 14, 15, 19, 21

Hidden Fear: "More ceremonies, POs lobbying us directly, zero time for tech debt, and management ranking our individual story points."

Your Pitch: "This framework shields you from chaos. First, POs cannot inject work directly to you—you only pull from the single Shared Ready Queue after items pass Definition of Ready. Second, we reserve 20% capacity for UAT support and 10% for Operations, and make Technical Enablers a formal Service Class. Third, individual velocity, lines of code, and AI prompt counts are strictly prohibited as management metrics."

📅 The 7 Ceremonies & Their Distinct Decision Purpose (Slides 16–17)

1. Quarterly Planning (Gate G3): Approve Outcomes, classify Committed / Forecast / Candidate, set 70/20/10 capacity, lock control windows.
2. Feature Refinement: Slice features into testable stories (Small/Medium), assign Route A, B, or C, confirm dependencies.
3. Backlog Replenishment (NEW — Chaired by SM): Weekly/Pre-Sprint gate filtering ready work via the 6-Step Policy Stack into the One Shared Ready Queue.
4. Sprint Planning: Set one primary Sprint Goal and pull conservative feasible work from the Shared Ready Queue.
5. Daily Flow Review: Walk the board Right-to-Left focusing on completion, Work-Item Age, blockers, and WIP limits (not "what I did yesterday").
6. Sprint Review: Inspect integrated working software and return stakeholder feedback to the ordered Product Backlog.
7. Retrospective: Inspect system flow/quality metrics and commit to 1–2 concrete system improvements.

📐 Progressive Estimation & The 6 Executive Decisions (Slides 14 & 23)

"Estimate informs forecast; forecast informs commitment; an estimate is never a commitment."
• Demand / Candidate (G1–G2): XS–XL + uncertainty rating (Discovery need)
• MVP / Quarter Goal (G3): Mandatory feature-group sizes + dependencies (Commitment)
• Feature / Enhancement: XS–XL + reference feature comparison (Trade-offs)
• User Story (G4): Right-sized Small / Medium / Too Large (Points optional)
• Delivery System Forecast: Empirical Throughput + Cycle-Time distributions
6 Management Decisions Requested on Slide 23:
1. Operating Model: Adopt Controlled Scrumban + DevOps + OHB Governance.
2. Roadmap Language: Adopt Committed / Forecast (or Stretch) / Pipeline Candidate.
3. Prioritization Model: Approve Option A (Executive Quarterly Order + Weekly Replenishment).
4. Capacity Policy: Approve 70/20/10 reserves + decide explicit Technical Enabler reserve.
5. Queue Governance: Approve One Shared Ready Queue, neutral SM facilitator & change rule.
6. Pilot Mandate: Authorize Sprint 0 + 1 representative product pilot.
